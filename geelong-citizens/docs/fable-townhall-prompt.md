# Prompt: designing a principled community forum (for Claude Fable)

Attach these files when you paste the prompt below:
- `FOUNDING.md` (the LocalRegister Founding Document)
- `geelong-citizens/README.md`
- `geelong-citizens/CLAUDE.md`

---

I'm one of a small group of residents in Geelong, Victoria (Australia). I'd like your best thinking on a design question. Take your time, challenge my assumptions, and be frank where you think we've got it wrong.

## Background

- **LocalRegister** (attached: FOUNDING.md) is our neutral, community-owned public record of what's been raised with authorities, what responses came back, and what's still waiting. It is pre-political: it records, it doesn't advocate. Nothing in it is ever deleted.
- **Geelong Citizens** (attached: README.md and CLAUDE.md) is a small web app we built to help residents write personal emails to their councillors and MPs. Every email comes from a real, verified person, who reviews it before it's sent. If AI is used, it only works from what that person wrote.
- **What prompted this:** the Corio Bay Miniature Railway has proposed a passenger-carrying miniature railway and community development at Haines Reserve in Hamlyn Heights. Residents opposed to it started an email campaign to councillors and MPs. From where we sat, it felt like an anti-campaign built partly on conjecture and personal attacks. Our instinct was to organise in kind, which is why we first called our side a "campaign". But we don't want to be the other side's mirror image. We want our side, and ideally the whole conversation, to be more transparent and verifiable, and grounded in real community debate.

## The question

How could we build a genuinely principled community forum or town hall that:

1. takes scattered, disconnected views (Facebook posts, emails, conversations, petitions) and turns them into a coherent, honest discussion where people can see the real points of agreement and disagreement;
2. separates claims that can be checked from opinions and values, and makes the evidence visible;
3. lets people be frank but discourages personal attacks and rumour, without becoming censorship;
4. treats everyone fairly, including people who oppose what we want, so the outcome is credible to councillors and to the wider community;
5. connects naturally to the email tool, so that what residents send their representatives is informed by the discussion, and to LocalRegister, so that what was raised and how representatives responded becomes part of the permanent record.

It has to work within reason and reality: a few volunteers in a regional city, little money, limited time.

## Things I'd like you to weigh honestly

- Whether a group with a stake in one outcome can credibly run a fair forum on the same issue. What would make that credible? Or should it be run separately, or co-run with the other side?
- How to verify that participants are real local residents without excluding people or creating privacy risks.
- Moderation that a few volunteers could actually sustain.
- Where AI genuinely helps (for example summarising, mapping arguments, flagging unverified claims) and where it would undermine trust. How could any AI summary stay traceable to what real people actually said?
- What has worked and failed elsewhere (for example deliberative polling, citizens' juries, vTaiwan and Polis, participatory budgeting, council "Have Your Say" sites), and what's realistic at our scale.
- How to avoid becoming a pile-on, an echo chamber or a ghost town.
- Australian legal realities a volunteer group should know about (defamation, privacy, electoral and advocacy rules), in general terms, flagging where we'd need proper advice.

## What I'd like back

A design proposal I can share with the others in our group:

- the core principles: short, plain English, in the spirit of our Founding Document
- how a discussion actually flows, from scattered views, to a coherent picture, to letters to representatives, to the public record, using Haines Reserve as the worked example
- the smallest version we could launch in a few weeks, and what to add later
- the biggest risks, and how we'd know if it's failing
- the questions our group needs to answer before building anything

Please write in plain English; I'm not a professional developer. Where you disagree with how we've framed this, say so.
